Platform engineering needs to become more human…and less

source: linkedin — original ↗
published: 2026-11-13 · status: canonical · expanded from the original post

If a platform rule only exists in someone’s memory, it isn’t ready for AI agents.

The useful detail in this CNCF article is the call for Kubernetes-style control loops, alongside machine-readable policies and scoped identities: https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/11/11/platform-engineering-needs-to-become-more-humanand-less/?utm_source=tldrit

Permissions define what an agent can touch. They don’t tell you whether the platform is still in an acceptable state after it acts.

Intent isn’t enforcement.

For teams moving agents into production, I’d treat reconciliation as a design requirement: define acceptable state, observe changes, and decide which deviations can be corrected automatically versus escalated to a human.

That also helps the engineer improvising under pressure.

The lesson: encode the boundaries people shouldn’t have to remember, so they can focus on decisions that still need judgment.
